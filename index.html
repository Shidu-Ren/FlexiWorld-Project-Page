01 / THE IDEAThink beyond a fixed step

A world model.
More than one tempo.

Goals can be far away. Actions do not have to come in one fixed length.

FlexiWorld learns latent dynamics and goal-conditioned action generation together, using variable-length action chunks and multiple goal spans. The same trained model can plan at different temporal resolutions.

05 / IN THE PAPERThe full picture

Abstract

Latent world models predict future states for goal-directed planning using action chunks spanning multiple primitive steps. Existing methods typically use fixed-length chunks and either omit goal-conditioned action generation or limit their supervision to short goal spans. We introduce FlexiWorld, a JEPA-based world model that combines long-horizon goal supervision with variable-length action chunks to improve long-horizon control. During training, we sample varying goal spans and randomly partition the actions into variable-length chunks. We jointly train the world model with a causal action encoder that embeds variable-length chunks and an autoregressive actor that generates primitive actions sequentially. Student Forcing reduces exposure bias by training on generated action prefixes. For planning, Actor-Residual Cross-Entropy Method (ARCEM) generalizes POPLIN's action-residual search with feedback within autoregressive chunks. Across four benchmarks and goal distances, FlexiWorld with ARCEM achieves 89.29% mean success, compared with 83.98% for the strongest baseline. PushT ablations show improved direct control from long-horizon supervision, variable-length chunks, and Student Forcing. Without retraining, FlexiWorld supports different planning chunk lengths: longer chunks accelerate ARCEM by approximately 1.3× while maintaining comparable average success.
